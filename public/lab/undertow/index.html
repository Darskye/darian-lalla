About

What you're seeing

Each thread of light is a tracer released into real surface currents. In Heat mode its colour is that day's sea-surface temperature, and it glows brighter where the water moves faster. The timeline plays 59 days of data, .

Speed mode colours by how fast the water moves. Spin mode shows eddies: blue water turns the same way as the Earth (cyclonic, usually a cold core), red turns against it (anticyclonic, usually a warm core).

How satellites measure currents

Radar altimeters on satellites measure the height of the sea surface to within a few centimetres. Warm eddies bulge up and cold ones dip down. Because the Earth rotates, water flows along those slopes instead of down them, so the shape of the surface gives the current. NOAA turns it into a daily current map on a 25 km grid.

Temperature comes from NOAA's daily OISST analysis, which blends satellite readings with ships and buoys.

What's measured and what's drawn

Currents, temperatures, coastlines and dates are measured. The tracers are a drawing tool: they run about ×50,000 faster than the water so that a day's pattern shows up in a second. A 25 km grid resolves eddies and jets, but it misses tides, wind drift and swirls smaller than about 50 km.
